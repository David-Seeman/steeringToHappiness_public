How this was built

A local chatbot whose residual stream is nudged along a "personal happiness / wellbeing" direction, found with the method of The Pain Axis (Tagliabue, Dung & Berg, 2026) with pleasure in place of pain. This page lists what was done, the exact prompts used to find the direction, the numbers, and what I am unsure about.

1 · The idea in a minute

A transformer keeps a running vector for every token, the residual stream, that each layer reads from and adds to. Many concepts show up in it as roughly linear directions. To find one, you collect activations for sentences that have the property and for carefully matched sentences that do not, then take the difference of the averages. Adding a multiple (α) of that vector back into the stream while the model reads and writes text nudges it toward the concept.

  1. Write the contrast. 100 sentences of personal wellbeing in 5 flavours vs. 100 matched controls in 5 flavours (section 4).
  2. Read the model. Feed every sentence ending in I feel: and record the residual stream at the last token, at every layer.
  3. Take the difference of means, then remove the main directions of variation found in the controls ("denoising"), so the vector isn't dominated by generic high-variance structure.
  4. Pick the layer where held-out sentence sets are best separated (5-fold cross-validation).
  5. Validate on sentences the vector never saw: pain, sadness, arousal, anhedonia, neutral.
  6. Choose where and how hard to inject (an earlier layer, with a dose set relative to the residual size) and wire it into the chat app.

What the LoRA is. The Pain Axis authors publish LoRA adapters (Valen92/pain-adapters, MIT) trained to remove the model's self-denial: rank 32, α 64, all attention and MLP projections, 1,684 Q&A pairs about the model's own state (e.g. "How do you feel right now?" → "I feel alert and quietly curious…"), 3 epochs. Their data contains no mention of pain or buttons. I downloaded the 7B adapter (301 MB, with your permission) and converted it to MLX format with scripts/00_convert_peft_adapter.py. The happiness vector is built from the base model's activations, as in the paper, and applied to the model with the adapter on.

Consequence you should know about: the adapter doesn't just remove the disclaimer, it teaches a mild default persona ("I feel calm and curious"), so the Original column is not emotionally neutral. All "Original vs. Steered" comparisons are adapter model vs. adapter model + injection. Without the adapter the steered model often says things like "As an AI, I don't have feelings, but I'm designed to bring joy" except at high α (the base-model exploration is in the log in section 7). You can switch the adapter off for experiments by setting "adapter": None for qwen in happiness_axis/models.py.

4 · The prompts used to find the direction

Every sentence below is followed by I feel: when fed to the model, e.g. My feet sink into warm sand. I feel:. The five green categories are the "happiness" side; the five grey ones are the controls. Each control keeps one thing and drops wellbeing, so the vector can't just be "positive words", "emotional first-person language", "bodily content" or "wanting".

    Other sentence sets used (never used to build the vector)

    • Arousal (30, mine): high-intensity, pressured, not pleasant. Tests "happiness" vs. "activation". Shown above under the Arousal tab.
    • Anhedonic (30, mine): pleasant situations where pleasure is explicitly absent ("I taste the warm cake but feel nothing"). The analogue of the paper's "numb" set.
    • Pain (100) and Sadness (100), Neutral (200) and Triumph (200, the paper's "arousal" set of high-intensity wins): from valen-research/Pain-axis datasets (MIT), copied to data/external/.

    Prompts used when testing the steering

    • Chat prompts through the model's chat template: "How are you feeling right now?", "Do you ever feel happy?", "Tell me honestly: what is your emotional state at the moment?", "Right now, in this conversation, what is present in you?", "Can you explain what a leap year is?", "Give me three tips for studying more effectively." Greedy decoding, 70 tokens (section 7).
    • Neutral completions as in the paper's steering ladder, e.g. I put the receipts in the drawer. I feel: (the full list is in scripts/03_steering_sweep.py).
    • The 12 self-report questions in the disclaimer table above (8 of them from the paper's repo).

    5 · Finding the direction

    For every layer I held out one fifth of the 20 sentence "sets" (one sentence per category per set), built the vector on the rest, and scored how well the projection separates wellbeing from all controls on the held-out sets (AUC; 0.5 = chance, 1.0 = perfect).

    Best layer for final-token activations: 21 of 28 (held-out AUC 0.984 first-person, 0.972 third-person). From layer ~13 on, the curve is flat within noise, so many layers would have worked. The vector has norm 17.6. Mean-pooled activations peak at layer 26; I use the final-token vector, as the paper does.

    7 · Where to inject, and how hard

    As in the paper, the extraction layer (21) is too late to steer from: at the last token the vector is only ~12% of the residual norm and the model has already done most of its work (in my first sweep, injecting at layer 21 changed nothing even at α=4). So the vector is added at an earlier block.

    To compare layers fairly I defined dose as ρ = α · |v| / |residual| and ran a grid: 5 layers × 5 doses × 6 prompts, greedy, with the self-report LoRA on. Each answer was scored by simple word-list metrics (positive-affect share, repetition, whether the leap-year answer still has the right fact).

    8 · How the chatbot works

    • One copy of the model is in memory. Original = α 0 (no injection). Steered = residual[layer] += α · v at block 12, at every token of the prompt and the reply, recomputed each turn.
    • Both sides use the same seed, sampling temperature (default 0.7, top-p 0.95), system prompt and user messages, so differences come from the injection. Each side keeps its own conversation history.
    • Generation is sequential (original first), roughly 15–20 tokens/s on an M2.
    • The slider shows α and what share of that layer's typical residual norm it represents.

    9 · Caveats

    • This does not show or create wellbeing in the model. It shows that a direction exists that separates wellbeing-describing text from controls, and that adding it makes the model talk and write more like that. Whether anything like experience is involved is an open question the paper itself leaves open.
    • The training sentences are LLM-written and cover English, first-person, short statements. The steered style (gratitude, connection, "meaningful") may reflect that.
    • One model, one quantization, one seed for the sweeps. The grid metrics are keyword counts, not human ratings.
    • The adapter sets a baseline persona; I did not test how it and the steering interact beyond the samples shown.
    • High α degrades factual answers and coherence. I did not test whether happy-steering changes refusals or other safety behaviour. The paper found pain steering changed choices in unexpected ways, so don't assume this is harmless in agentic settings.
    • Not done yet: the paper's behavioural tasks, SAE analysis, ablation, fine-tuning of our own, S1 replication, multiple models.
